## Quick answer

Psyllium husk, also sold as ispaghula husk, is a soluble fibre made from the seed coat of the *Plantago ovata* plant. It is widely sold over the counter as a laxative for constipation.

- **It is a bulk-forming laxative.** It soaks up water in the gut and swells, making stools softer and bulkier so they are easier to pass.
- **The evidence is reasonable but not perfect.** In trials, fibre supplements helped more people with long-term constipation than placebo, and psyllium had the strongest evidence. The trials were small and had design weaknesses.
- **It is not instant.** The NHS says it takes 2 to 3 days to work.
- **Water matters.** Each dose needs to be mixed with plenty of liquid, and you should drink well through the day.

## What the research says

**A 2016 meta-analysis of fibre supplements.** Christodoulides and colleagues pooled 7 randomised controlled trials involving 430 adults with chronic constipation that had no obvious underlying cause. In the four trials that reported how many people improved, 77% of those taking fibre responded to treatment, compared with 44% of those taking a placebo. Fibre also increased how often people went to the toilet and made stools softer.

Psyllium stood out. One trial of almost 200 people found people taking psyllium were about 1.8 times as likely to respond as those taking placebo, and the psyllium trials as a group showed clear benefits for stool frequency and consistency. The authors concluded that psyllium had the most robust evidence of the fibres studied. However, they also rated the overall quality of the evidence as low, because all the trials had a high risk of bias, and they called for larger, better-designed studies.

**What doctors' guidelines say.** In 2023, the American Gastroenterological Association and the American College of Gastroenterology published a joint guideline on chronic constipation. It suggests fibre supplements, such as psyllium, bran or inulin, for adults with chronic constipation. This is a conditional recommendation based on low-certainty evidence. In plain terms, fibre is a sensible first step, but the research behind it is limited.

**The side effect seen in trials.** The 2016 meta-analysis found that fibre increased wind (flatulence), and bloating also tended to be higher. This matches NHS advice that wind and bloating are the most common side effects, especially at first.

Overall, the evidence rating here is **moderate**: results point consistently in the same direction, but the trials are few and not high quality.

## How to take it

Follow your product's instructions, as the amount of psyllium varies between powders, granules, capsules and wafers. General advice from the NHS and MedlinePlus:

1. **Mix it well with liquid.** For sachets of granules, the NHS advises stirring the contents into at least 150 ml of cold water and drinking it straight away. MedlinePlus advises mixing powder into a full 240 ml glass of liquid, such as water or fruit juice, and says each dose should be taken with at least that much fluid.
2. **Typical adult dosing.** For the common UK sachet product, the NHS dose for adults and children aged 12 and over is one sachet twice a day. Other products differ, so check the label.
3. **Take it after a meal or snack.** This is the NHS timing advice.
4. **Not right before bed.** Take the evening dose at least an hour before going to sleep. The NHS says taking it just before sleep can make it harder for the bowel to move stools along.
5. **Avoid breathing in the powder.** Both the NHS and MedlinePlus note that inhaling psyllium dust can rarely cause an allergic reaction.
6. **Drink plenty through the day.** The NHS suggests adults aim for more than 2 litres (about 8 glasses) of fluid daily. Without enough fluid, constipation can get worse rather than better.
7. **Be patient, but not too patient.** Expect results in 2 to 3 days. If things have not improved or have got worse after 3 days, speak to a doctor or pharmacist. MedlinePlus advises not using it for more than a week unless a doctor recommends it. For longer-term problems, a doctor may suggest taking it for months.

Psyllium works best alongside the basics: eating more fibre from food, staying active and not putting off going to the toilet when you feel the urge.

> Safety note: Take every dose of psyllium with a full glass of liquid. If you have trouble swallowing, throat problems, or symptoms that might mean a bowel blockage, speak to a doctor before using it.

## Possible side effects and interactions

**Common side effects.** Wind and bloating are the most common. The NHS says they happen because of the sudden increase in fibre and usually improve after a few days. If they continue or worry you, talk to a pharmacist.

**Allergic reactions.** Mild reactions such as itchy eyes, nose or skin are possible. Stop taking it and ask a pharmacist for advice if this happens. Swelling of the lips, mouth or throat, a tight throat or difficulty breathing or swallowing need emergency help straight away.

**Effects on other medicines.** Psyllium can get in the way of other medicines if they are taken together. The NHS advises taking it at least 30 minutes before or after any other medicine. MedlinePlus is stricter for some drugs: it says not to take digoxin (a heart medicine), aspirin and other salicylates, or the antibiotic nitrofurantoin within 3 hours of psyllium.

If you take any regular medicines, vitamins or supplements, tell your pharmacist or doctor before starting psyllium, as some doses may need adjusting or closer monitoring. Do not combine it with another laxative unless a doctor or pharmacist suggests it.

## Who should be careful

Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using psyllium if you:

- Have difficulty swallowing or problems with your throat.
- Have stomach pain together with nausea or vomiting, which could be a sign of a bowel blockage.
- Have had a bowel blockage, or weak bowel muscles (colonic atony).
- Have had blood in your stools, or a change in how often you go that has lasted more than 2 weeks.
- Have diabetes, heart disease, high blood pressure or kidney disease, or follow a low-sugar or low-salt diet. MedlinePlus lists all of these as reasons to check first.
- Have phenylketonuria, a rare inherited condition that the NHS lists as a reason to check first.
- Have reacted badly to psyllium before.

The NHS says most adults can take it, including during pregnancy and breastfeeding, but MedlinePlus advises checking with your doctor if you are pregnant or breastfeeding. Children under 6 should only take it if a doctor prescribes it.

## When to see a doctor

Get medical advice if:

- Your constipation has not improved, or has got worse, after 3 days of taking psyllium.
- You notice blood in your stools.
- Your bowel habits change and the change lasts longer than 2 weeks.
- You have stomach pain with nausea or vomiting, which can be a sign of a blockage.
- You find you need psyllium for more than a week to keep going. MedlinePlus advises longer use only on a doctor's advice.

Call emergency services straight away if your lips, mouth, throat or tongue swell, your throat feels tight, or you are struggling to breathe or swallow after taking it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does psyllium take to work?

The NHS says bulk-forming laxatives such as ispaghula husk take 2 to 3 days to work. They are not designed for fast relief. If you still have no improvement after 3 days, speak to a doctor or pharmacist.

### Can I take psyllium every day?

For short bouts of constipation, it is meant to be used for a short time. MedlinePlus advises not using it for more than a week without medical advice. A doctor may recommend longer use for ongoing constipation or as a fibre supplement, so ask if you think you need it regularly.

### Is psyllium better than other laxatives?

Not necessarily. The NHS says the main types of laxative (bulk-forming, osmotic and stimulant) work in different ways and are similarly effective. Stimulant laxatives such as senna usually work faster, often overnight. The best choice depends on how hard your stools are and how difficult they are to pass, so a pharmacist can help you choose.

### Why does psyllium make me bloated?

The sudden increase in fibre can cause wind and bloating, which trials and NHS advice both confirm. This usually eases within a few days. The NHS also suggests raising fibre in your diet gradually, aiming for about 30 g a day for adults, and drinking plenty of water.
